How to make your cards

  1. Keep 1–75 numbers selected, or switch to 1–90 or your own words.
  2. Choose how many cards you need (one per player, plus a few spares) and how many go on each page.
  3. Pick US Letter or A4, then press Print or save as PDF. To get a PDF, choose Save as PDF as the printer.
  4. Keep the set code. Typing it in again later gives you exactly the same cards, so you can print more copies or check a winning card against the original.

How to play 75-ball bingo

Each card has five columns under the letters B, I, N, G and O. Column B holds numbers from 1 to 15, I from 16 to 30, N from 31 to 45, G from 46 to 60 and O from 61 to 75. The centre square is free, so it counts as marked from the start.

The caller draws numbers one at a time and reads them out with their letter, for example “B 7” or “G 52”. Players mark the number if it is on their card. The caller ticks each number off on the caller sheet, so a winning card can be checked.

Agree on the winning pattern before you start. The usual ones are:

  • A line: five in a row across, down or diagonally (the free square counts).
  • Four corners: the four corner squares.
  • Blackout (or coverall): every square on the card.

The first player with the pattern shouts “Bingo!”, and the caller checks the card against the caller sheet.

Drawing the numbers

You do not need a bingo machine. Cut the caller sheet into 75 slips and draw them from a bowl, or keep the sheet whole and use a random number generator, ticking off each number as it is called.

Frequently asked questions

How many cards should I print?

One per player, plus a few spares. Players can also play two or three cards at once.

Are all the cards different?

Yes. Each card in a set is dealt separately from the set code, so two cards in a set are almost never the same.

What is the difference between 75-ball and 90-ball bingo?

75-ball is the American game on a 5×5 card. 90-ball is the British game on a ticket with 3 rows and 9 columns. You can make 90-ball tickets here too.

Is it free?

Yes. There is no sign-up and no watermark, and you can print as many cards as you like.

Can I save the cards as a PDF?

Yes. Press Print or save as PDF and choose Save as PDF (or Microsoft Print to PDF) as the printer. The PDF can then be emailed or printed somewhere else.

Is what I type sent anywhere?

No. The cards are made in your browser. Your word list never leaves your device.
