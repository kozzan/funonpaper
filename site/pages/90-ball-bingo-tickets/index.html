How a 90-ball ticket works

A ticket has 3 rows and 9 columns. Each row holds exactly five numbers, so a ticket has 15 numbers and 12 blank squares. The first column holds numbers from 1 to 9, the second from 10 to 19, and so on up to the last column, which holds 80 to 90. Within a column the numbers go up from top to bottom, which makes them quick to find.

How to play

The caller draws the numbers from 1 to 90 one at a time. Players mark each number that is on their ticket. A game usually has three prizes, played in order:

  • One line: all five numbers on one row.
  • Two lines: any two rows on the same ticket.
  • Full house: all 15 numbers.

The game goes on after each win until someone has a full house. The caller sheet lists every number from 1 to 90 to tick off, so a claim can be checked.

Frequently asked questions

How many tickets fit on a page?

One, three or six. Six per page gives the classic strip of tickets, and they are smaller but still easy to mark.

Does a strip of six cover every number?

Not here. Each ticket is dealt on its own, so a number can appear on more than one ticket of a page and some numbers on none.

Can I play with 75 numbers instead?

Yes. The American game uses a 5×5 card with numbers 1 to 75: see printable bingo cards.

Is it free?

Yes. There is no sign-up and no watermark, and you can print as many cards as you like.

Can I save the cards as a PDF?

Yes. Press Print or save as PDF and choose Save as PDF (or Microsoft Print to PDF) as the printer. The PDF can then be emailed or printed somewhere else.

Is what I type sent anywhere?
